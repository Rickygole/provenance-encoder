VERDICT (composition): the shift toward English in the candidate pool has TWO
distinct pieces -- moving from MISSING to ENGLISH raises P(candidate) sharply
(0.097 -> 0.208, n=4563 vs 4234), while
RECORDED_NON_ENGLISH yield (0.167, n=986) is close to ENGLISH and much higher than MISSING.
Given Exp2a: the RECORDED_NON_ENGLISH gap fully resolves to volume (OR->0.94, ns after
adjustment); the MISSING gap is attenuated but NOT resolved by volume (OR 0.41->0.64,
still p<0.001). So the composition shift is driven predominantly by MISSING-language
admissions (an administrative-completeness pattern), not by recorded non-English
admissions (a language-per-se pattern) -- and even the missingness piece is only
partly a volume artifact.
