  A nursing note and a discharge summary can disagree about a medication,
and only the summary leaves the hospital.
This thesis builds a four stage pipeline for such contradictions
in an intensive care database and audits each
stage. First, the first three stages reported a number their check did
not support: of three credited architectural components, one parameter is an
initialisation artifact, one metric had its target in its input, one module
was never trained. Second, a pre-training check applies the classical
fixed-marginal agreement bound to a weak labeller: flagging 261 of
299 pairs, against 12 a pharmacist confirmed, caps its Matthews correlation
at 0.0780; measured, it is 0.0269. Third, a mining bug class,
trigger-exemplar dissociation, where displayed evidence is picked by position,
not from the triggering set, caught by a one line assertion; reachable
here, though its frequency is unestablished. With 12 positives,
the evaluation resolves only large effects on area under the curve, about
0.75 to 0.81. The detector was indistinguishable from chance on the 253
non-ambiguous pairs; on the 108 Rules 3 and 4 pairs, which the documented
selection defect does not affect, its area of 0.6095 (95 percent interval
0.3029 to 0.9010) leaves open whether the null is a defect artifact. By
average precision, chosen post hoc, a bag-of-words model and a model never
trained on the weak labels beat their chance baselines, unlike the detector,
though no paired test separates them. The work does not show the task
unsolvable.
