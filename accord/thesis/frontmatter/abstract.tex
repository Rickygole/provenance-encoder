  A nursing note and a discharge summary can disagree about a medication,
and only the summary leaves the hospital.
This thesis builds a four stage pipeline for such contradictions
in an intensive care database, checking every stage with a control or code audit. It
makes three contributions. First, the first three stages each reported a
number their control or code audit did not support; of three credited
architectural components, one parameter is an initialisation artifact, one
metric had its target in its input, one module was never trained.
Second, a check that can be run before training: a weak labeller flagging
261 of 299 pairs, against 12 a pharmacist confirmed, fixes from counts alone
a ceiling of 0.0780 on the Matthews correlation any thresholded label could
reach. Third, a mining bug class, trigger-exemplar dissociation, where
displayed evidence is picked by position, not from the triggering set,
caught by a one line assertion. With 12 positives, the evaluation resolves
only large effects on area under the curve, about 0.75 to 0.81. The trained
detector was indistinguishable from chance on the 253 non-ambiguous pairs;
on the 108 pairs free of the selection defect its area of 0.6095 (95 percent
interval 0.3029 to 0.9010) leaves open whether the null is a defect
artifact. By average precision, chosen post hoc, a bag-of-words model and a
model never trained on the weak labels beat their chance baselines, unlike the
detector, yet no paired test separates the three. The work does not
show the task unsolvable.
