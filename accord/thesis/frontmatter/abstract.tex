  {\doublespacing\RaggedRight\parindent=0.5in\RaggedRightParindent=0.5in
  A nursing note and a discharge summary can disagree about a medication, and only the summary leaves
the hospital. This thesis audits this project's own earlier readings of a four-stage pipeline
detecting such contradictions in an intensive care database. Three of the four stage readings
did not survive their checks (one not established, two unsupported); the fourth, the evaluation's near-chance reading, survived as a bounded null; one
annotator leaves it fragile. Candidate precision is not established; credited architectural
components are unsupported by code audit. A detector ensemble retrained on subject-disjoint rows is
indistinguishable from chance on 253 non-ambiguous pairs, as is the plain control detector retrained
on reproducible labels. Those pairs resolve only area under the curve effects above 0.7466 under
one test, 0.8084 under another. A feasibility gate, applied after training, finds that a
labeller flagging 261 of 299 pairs, against 12 a pharmacist confirmed, left no outcome able to
reject chance agreement (smallest attainable one-sided significance 0.1894); the measured
agreement did not. A failed gate says this pilot could not have tested the labeller; passing it is no evidence the labeller works.
Trigger-exemplar dissociation is a bug pattern observed once here: the original positional selector
shows an exemplar outside the trigger set for 41.4 percent of the rebuilt pool's Rules 1 and 2
candidates, a design rate that leaves the annotated exemplars' defect rate unresolved. Before
training, builders should measure a labeller's positive rate on a small expert-labelled pilot.
Unsolvability is not shown.
