  A nursing note and a discharge summary can disagree about a medication,
and only the summary leaves the hospital.
This thesis builds and audits a four stage pipeline detecting such
contradictions in an intensive care database.
First, no stage's reported number survived its check: one not established,
two unsupported, one a bounded null. Candidate precision is not established;
the weak labeller's recall rests on chance agreement; credited
architectural components are an initialisation artifact, a metric with its
target in its input and an untrained module; the detector is
indistinguishable from chance on 253 non-ambiguous pairs, resolving only area
under the curve effects above 0.75 to 0.81. Second, a check that can be
run before training, applied post hoc here: a labeller flagging 261 of 299
pairs, against 12 a pharmacist confirmed, could not have rejected chance, its
smallest attainable one-sided significance being 0.1894. Third,
trigger-exemplar dissociation, a reachable mining bug pattern: displayed
evidence is picked by position, not from the triggering set; its rate is
unrecoverable, instruments giving 0 to 0.9315, because the schema never
recorded which mention fired; a one line assertion would catch it once it
does. On the 108 Rules 3 and 4 pairs, outside the documented selection defect,
exemplar correctness assumed, not verified, the detector's area of 0.6095
(interval 0.3029 to 0.9010) leaves open whether the null is a defect artifact.
Post hoc, by average precision, two comparison models beat chance where the
detector does not, no paired test separating them. Unsolvability is not
shown.
