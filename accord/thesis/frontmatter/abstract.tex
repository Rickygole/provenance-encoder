  A nursing note and a discharge summary can disagree about a medication,
and only the summary leaves the hospital.
This thesis builds and audits a four stage pipeline detecting such
contradictions in an intensive care database.
First, no stage's reported reading survived its check: one not established,
two unsupported, one a bounded null. Candidate precision is not established;
the weak labeller's recall rests on chance agreement; credited architectural
components are an initialisation artifact, a metric with its target in its
input and an untrained module; a detector ensemble retrained on
subject-disjoint rows is indistinguishable from chance on 253 non-ambiguous
pairs, resolving only area under the curve effects above 0.75 to 0.81. Its
area under the curve there is indistinguishable from its weak labeller's
(0.5145 against 0.5185), consistent with a student reproducing a teacher at
chance. Second, a check runnable before training was applied after the fact:
a labeller flagging 261 of 299 pairs, against 12 a pharmacist confirmed, could
not have rejected chance, its smallest attainable one-sided significance being
0.1894. Third, trigger-exemplar dissociation is a reachable mining bug pattern:
displayed evidence is picked by position, not from the triggering set. Its
rate is unrecoverable, instruments giving 0 to 0.9315, because the schema never
recorded which mention fired; a one line assertion would catch it once it
does. Builders of such pipelines should measure the labeller's positive
rate and agreement on a small expert-labelled sample before training.
Unsolvability is not shown.
