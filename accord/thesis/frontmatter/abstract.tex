  A nursing note and a discharge summary can disagree about a medication,
and only the summary leaves the hospital.
This thesis builds a standard four stage pipeline for such contradictions
in an intensive care database and audits every stage with a control. It
makes three contributions. First, the audit finds each stage's number
unsupported by its control; of three credited architectural components, one
parameter is an initialisation artifact, one metric vacated by label leakage, one module never trained.
Second, a check run before training: a weak labeller flagging 261 of 299
pairs, against 12 a pharmacist confirmed, fixes from counts alone a ceiling
of 0.0780 on the Matthews correlation any thresholded label could reach.
Third, a mining bug class, trigger-exemplar dissociation, where displayed
evidence is picked by position, not from the triggering set, caught by a
one line assertion. With 12 positives, a power analysis shows
the evaluation resolves only large effects on area under the curve,
about 0.75 to 0.81. Within it, the trained detector was not
distinguishable from chance on the 253 pairs without an ambiguous label; on
the 108 pairs free of the selection defect its area of 0.6095 (95 percent
interval 0.3029 to 0.9010) cannot settle whether the null is a defect
artifact. By average precision, chosen after its value was known, a
bag-of-words model found a small surface lexical signal the detector did
not, surviving 11 of 12 leave-one-positive-out drops. The work does not show
the task unsolvable.
