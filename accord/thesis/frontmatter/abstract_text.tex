A nursing note and a discharge summary can disagree about a medication, and only the summary leaves
the hospital. This thesis audits this project's earlier readings of a four-stage pipeline for
detecting such contradictions in intensive care records. Three of the four stage readings did not
survive their checks (one not established, two unsupported); the fourth, the evaluation's near-chance
reading, survived as a bounded null. One annotator leaves it fragile: 1 or 2 targeted relabels undo the bound. Candidate precision is not
established, and credited architectural components are unsupported by code audit. A detector ensemble
retrained on subject-disjoint rows is indistinguishable from chance on 253 non-ambiguous pairs, as is a
control retrained on reproducible labels. Those pairs resolve only area under the curve
effects above 0.7466 or 0.8084, depending on the test. A feasibility gate applied after training shows
that a labeller flagging 261 of 299 pairs, against 12 a pharmacist confirmed, could not have rejected
chance agreement at any outcome (smallest attainable one-sided significance 0.1894), and it did not. A
failed gate says this pilot could not have tested the labeller; passing it is no evidence the labeller
works. Trigger-exemplar dissociation is a bug pattern observed once: on the rebuilt pool the original
positional selector shows an exemplar outside the trigger set for 41.4 percent of Rules 1 and 2
candidates, a design rate that leaves the annotated exemplars' defect rate unresolved. Before
training, builders should measure a labeller's positive rate on an expert-labelled pilot.
Unsolvability is not shown.
