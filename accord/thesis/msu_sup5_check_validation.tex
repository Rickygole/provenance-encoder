\section{Validation of This Thesis's Own Checks}
\label{sup:s5}

This section reports whether the checks this thesis relies on elsewhere
actually work: whether they fire on a known defect and stay quiet on a clean
run, what a power analysis on a real score distribution at this project's
own evaluation size says each decision rule can resolve, and whether a
sweep run in public replicates the identity it is supposed to satisfy at
every cell rather than only in aggregate. Every subsection states whether its
own experiment was pre-registered, the outcome letter its pre-registration's
unchanged decision rule assigns, and what the result does and does not
license.

\subsection{C102: The Planted-Defect Benchmark}
\label{sup:sec:s5-c102}
% Artifact: results/accord/plantbench/{stage1_s1b,stage2_s2abc,stage3_registered,stage4_s4}.json.
% Register key prefix: plantbench.*

C102 is pre-registered and tests whether four of this project's own checks fire on a planted defect
and stay quiet on a clean run, at this project's own gold size ($n=253$).
Stage 1 (reduced) and Stage 2 ran as registered. Stages 3 and 4 ran with
untrained weights rather than the trained detector the registration
specifies, and are Outcome C for those stages (below). The two checks whose
verdicts this thesis relies on are S2c and S3d.

\textbf{Stage 1, S1b (a synthetic mirror-image check of group disagreement).} A synthetic
$253$-candidate population is split proportionally into group A (Rules 1 to 2, $139$ candidates,
$7$ of the $12$ true positives) and group B (Rules 3 to 4, $114$ candidates, $5$ positives), and
tested one-sided. Group B is a synthetic construction, not the Rules 3 and 4 stratum used elsewhere in
this thesis ($134$ pairs with $6$ positives, or $108$ with $6$ once ambiguous pairs are dropped). That
stratum is not subject to the documented Rules 1/2 positional defect; its exemplar correctness in the
gold set is assumed, not verified, because the original pipeline's Rules 3/4 selector code does not survive.
An exemplar-failure rate is planted in group B only, at two severities. Table~\ref{sup:tab:s5-c102-s1}
gives the verdict at each.

S1b does not clear the general verdict rule's sensitivity bar at either
planted severity at this sample size, so it is not validated at this sample
size. It mirrors, and does not validate, the Chapter 4 comparison between rule groups,
which is two-sided on $299$ pairs ($165$ with $6$ positives against $134$ with $6$) and is
confounded with rule content and base rate, so it is not a test of the defect;
the instrument limit of that comparison is carried by its own power at the observed rates,
$0.0495$, not by S1b. That power is computed by exact enumeration: its committed generator sums
the two binomial probabilities over every outcome pair on which the two-sided Fisher exact test
rejects at $0.05$, and reports $0.094$ as the smallest excess of the Rules 1/2 rate over the base
rate that reaches $80\%$ power.

\textbf{S2c is validated.} It fires on clean and on L1 (both retain real
signal between the score and the observed label) at 20 of 20, and stays
quiet on L2 (pure noise) at 0 of 20, exactly the design in the
pre-registration.

\textbf{S2b is reported under both readings the committed rule leaves open},
because the pre-registration states S2b's fire condition but not which arms
are its plant and which is its clean arm. Under reading 1 (L1 and L2 are the
plant-side arms, clean is the clean arm), S2b is validated: clean fires 0 of
20 and both L1 and L2 fire 20 of 20. Under reading 2 (clean and L1 are the
plant-side arms, matching the design note under S2c, L2 is the clean arm),
S2b is unvalidated\_false\_alarm: L2 (the clean arm under this reading) fires
20 of 20, far above the false-alarm bar, even though clean itself (a
plant-side arm under this reading) also misses the sensitivity bar at 0 of
20. Because the two readings disagree, S2b carries no verdict this thesis
relies on.

\textbf{Stages 3 and 4 departed from their registration.} The registration specifies
the frozen detector with its trained weights for Stage 3 and that trained
detector's scores for Stage 4. Both stages were run with untrained, randomly
initialised weights instead. Three of the four Stage 3 checks, the normalisation
displacement, the shortcut gradient ratio and the rule-feature ablation, are
defined on behaviour only a trained model can exhibit, and Stage 4 received
scores that carry no signal by construction, so their failures to fire are not
evidence about the checks: they carry no verdict, and nothing in the thesis
relies on them. The fourth Stage 3 check asks only whether any gradient reaches
the harm head when its loss is excluded, a property of the computation graph
that holds without training; it fired on $20$ of $20$ plant replicates and
$0$ of $20$ clean replicates, and is the one detector check this benchmark
validates, with that caveat. Under the
registered rule these two stages are Outcome C, incomplete as registered.

\subsection{C101: Outcome C}
\label{sup:sec:s5-c101}
% Artifact: accord/public_controls/C101_PREREGISTRATION.md. No sizectl.* keys
% are live in the register (the run stands as invalid; register.py was not
% run on it), so this subsection carries no numbers.

C101 is pre-registered and built a positive control: a real, independently-known-good discriminator
(RoBERTa-large-MNLI on SNLI) subsampled to this project's exact gold shape,
to trace out the evaluation instrument's own empirical power curve. The run
is Outcome C, invalid, and stays invalid: no result from it is retracted or
patched in place, and no number from it is reported here or anywhere in this
thesis, because none passed the pre-registration's own validity check.

The invalidity is not a property of the instrument or of the discriminator
scored. It traces to a single calibration helper, the search that finds the
noise level realizing a target AUROC under the noise-mix construction. That
helper accepted a boundary value from a single noisy simulation draw without
ever entering the bisection loop that checks the search landed within
tolerance, so one grid cell (the noise-mix construction's target of chance
performance) was calibrated against a target it was never actually searched
for. Every other cell in the run calibrated within tolerance; only this one
early-return path produced an off-target construction. Per the
pre-registration's own kill condition, a run already seen is not patched and
silently rerun on top of itself: the fix belongs to a new pre-registration,
not an edit to this one, and C108 below is that corrected rerun, not a
replacement or a correction of C101 in place.

\subsection{C106: The Direct Exemplar Check}
\label{sup:sec:s5-c106}
% Artifact: results/accord/exemplar_direct/exemplar_direct.json.
% Register key prefix: exemplardirect.*

C106 is pre-registered and applies this project's own mention extractor and action classifier, fresh,
to the sentences the pharmacist actually saw, to measure the exemplar-selection
defect rate as a third instrument beside the two irreconcilable readings
Chapter 4 of the thesis reports ($0$ from a $23$-row key join, $0.4745$ from
a $137$-row reconstruction).

Of the $165$ pooled Rule 1 and Rule 2 candidates, $146$ are decidable under
this instrument (both sides' target drug found in the displayed, truncated
sentence). Among the $146$ decidable rows, $136$ are called wrong: a defect
rate of $0.9315$, with a Wilson 95\% interval that excludes both $0$ and
$0.4745$. Excluding the $15$ rows whose discharge cell sits at the
$200$-character cap, $137$ decidable rows remain and the rate is $0.9416$
($129$ of $137$), an interval that again excludes both existing readings:
capping is not what drives the high rate, since removing
the capped rows moves the rate up, not down.

This instrument's interval excludes both $0$ and $0.4745$, so it does not
settle the conflict between the two existing readings; per the
pre-registration's own rule this is \textbf{Outcome B}, does not settle it,
and it lands well above both rather than between them. The rate is reported
as extractor-dependent, not as ground truth: this check runs the extractor on
one isolated, frequently truncated sentence with no neighboring sentence,
materially harder than either existing reading's own context window, so the
gap is plausibly instrument artifact rather than a true rate three times the
reconstruction's. No row-level agreement with either existing reading is
attempted, because no committed artifact retains which of the $165$
\texttt{pair\_id}s belong to either of the two existing subsets.

\subsection{The Public-Sweep Identity Failures}
\label{sup:sec:s5-identity}
% Artifact: results/accord/stats/overcall_decomposition.json.
% Register key prefixes: overcalldecomp.* and sizectl108.*

This check is a committed regression test, not a pre-registered experiment:
it verifies the algebraic identity
$\mathrm{over\_call} = \mathrm{TPR} + \mathrm{FPR}\cdot(1-p)/p$ against the
public prevalence-sweep artifact's own confusion counts, at both the nominal
prevalence the sweep targeted and the realized prevalence its decided subset
actually has.

Of the 24 public-sweep cells, 6 fail the identity under the nominal target
prevalence; 0 fail it under the realized, decided-subset prevalence, which is
a tautology and would signal a code defect if it ever failed. All 6 failing
cells share one property: every one of them is a \texttt{B\_baserate\_abstain}
prompt cell, never an \texttt{A\_bare} cell. The reason is that abstention is
not label-neutral: the abstain-eligible prompt withholds an answer at a
different rate for true positives than for true negatives, so the decided
subset's realized prevalence drifts away from the nominal target the
publicly reported over-call ratio was computed by dividing by. An identity
that holds by construction under a prompt with no abstention option, and can
fail under nominal prevalence only where abstention is offered, is evidence
about the abstention mechanism, not about the identity itself, which holds
exactly once the realized prevalence is used in its place.

\subsection{The Trigger-Exemplar Assertion on Synthetic Candidates}
\label{sup:sec:s5-assertion}

Chapter 1 names trigger-exemplar dissociation and says a one-line assertion, that the displayed
exemplar belongs to the set of mentions that fired the rule, would catch it once the candidate
schema records that set. The assertion was run on two seeded synthetic fixtures of $1{,}000$
candidates each, holding one to six abstract mentions. In the first, each candidate has one
triggering mention at a random position ($515$ with the trigger first); a positional selector,
reproducing the original first-row pick, fails the assertion on $485$ candidates and a selector
restricted to the trigger set fails on $0$. In the second, the trigger set holds one to three
mentions ($456$, $308$ and $236$ candidates, $628$ with the first mention in the set); the positional
selector fails on $372$ and the trigger-restricted selector on $0$. That the assertion never fires on
any member of a trigger set is a construction check, verified over every member, not a finding. The
fixtures carry no clinical text and are released with unit tests. They show that the check
discriminates as intended; they do not measure how often the defect occurred in this project's
mining output, which the schema did not record.
