\section*{ABSTRACT}

\textbf{Title of the Thesis:} WHY CROSS-NOTE MEDICATION CONTRADICTION DETECTION
FAILS: A CONTROLLED FAILURE ANALYSIS OF A CLINICAL NLP PIPELINE

Ricky Gole, Master of Science in [DEGREE]

[MONTH YEAR]

\textbf{Thesis Chair:} [CHAIR NAME], Ph.D., Department of Computer Science

A contradiction between a nursing note and a discharge summary can carry a medication instruction out of the hospital that the care team had already stopped. This thesis builds the pipeline a competent group would build to detect such contradictions in a large intensive care database, combining rule-based candidate mining, a large language model used as a weak annotator, and a purpose-built neural detector. The pipeline does not work. Rather than report that as one negative result, this thesis runs at each stage the control that separates a real failure from an artifact of measurement. The candidate pool carries a true contradiction rate near five in one hundred, and among candidates whose evidence could be checked, sixty-five of one hundred and thirty-seven were justified by a sentence that did not contain the conflict. The weak annotator marks contradictions at fifteen to twenty-four times the expert rate across three models. The detector flags two hundred and twenty-five of two hundred and fifty-three annotated pairs and ranks no better than chance. A positive control, adjudicated by a rule committed before the data existed, shows the same procedure resolves a difference of four and a half accuracy points on a public benchmark where one exists, establishing that the clinical reading is a measurement rather than an absence of power. Three architectural components were falsified by controls that produced positive readings rather than silence. The work does not show the task is unsolvable. It shows where this pipeline fails and what each control cost.
