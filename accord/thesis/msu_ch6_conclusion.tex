\chapter{Summary and Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{What Was Asked}
\label{sec:conc-asked}

A nursing note records that a loop diuretic was held because the patient had
become hypovolemic; the discharge summary written five days later instructs
that the same drug be continued at the admission dose. Only one of those
documents leaves the hospital with the patient, and it is not the one
containing the reason the instruction is wrong.

This thesis asked whether that disagreement can be detected automatically
from paired clinical notes in MIMIC-III \citep{johnson2016mimic}, and built a
standard-shaped pipeline to answer it: rule-based candidate mining, language
model weak supervision, and a purpose-built neural detector over a
domain-pretrained clinical encoder. Each stage's reading was checked, and Section~\ref{sec:conc-found} states what survived that testing.

\section{What Was Found}
\label{sec:conc-found}

With 12 pharmacist-confirmed contradictions in 299 pairs, this evaluation
resolves only large effects: the analytic minimum detectable AUROC is 0.7355,
and a power analysis on real score distributions puts 80 percent power at
0.7466 for a two-sided Mann-Whitney test and 0.8084 for the registered
decision rule (Section~\ref{sec:res-rq4}). Within that limit, local language-model
labellers flagged far more pairs than the pharmacist confirmed: the deployed
labeller called 261 of the 299 pairs contradictions against 12 confirmed, and
three local models under the replication prompt
over-call the same pool by a factor of 15.8196 to 23.5959 against the
confirmed rate (Section~\ref{sec:res-rq2}). How much more depended heavily on
the prompt: a clinical-pharmacist framing prompt, whose differences from a
plain yes/no prompt are confounded, gave higher positive rates for
granite3.3:8b and qwen3:8b and lower for llama3:8b. Counting the 46 ambiguous
pairs as confirmed is a post hoc rescaling that multiplies every ratio by
12/58 and says nothing about agreement; the labellers did not flag ambiguous
pairs more than clear negatives. The margin is also fragile: relabelling 5
flagged pairs would bring the lowest hard-label ratio, 4.8333, below the
highest ratio measured on public text.

The detector trained on those labels was not distinguishable from chance.
On the 253 non-ambiguous pairs the decontaminated ensemble reaches an AUROC
of 0.5145, its bootstrap interval excluding values above 0.7034. On the 108
non-ambiguous Rules 3 and 4 pairs the exemplar-selection defect of
Section~\ref{sec:r-exemplar} cannot reach, the same ensemble reaches 0.6095
($p = 0.1910$); with 6 positives and a minimum detectable AUROC of 0.8058 this
fails to show the null is a defect artifact and cannot establish that it is
not. A bag-of-words model trained on the weak labels and an inference model
never trained on them each find a small precision signal the detector lacks:
the bag-of-words average precision, a statistic chosen after its value was
known, clears its no-skill line at $p = 0.0109$ on the full annotated set and
$p = 0.0159$ on the non-ambiguous set, and the two models are statistically
indistinguishable. That is a surface lexical signal, not evidence that the
weak labels carried signal.

\section{What This Establishes, and What It Does Not}
\label{sec:conc-establishes}

The labeller's own marginal fixes an agreement ceiling before a single pair
is scored: holding the confirmed and flagged counts fixed, the largest
Matthews correlation any thresholded label at that marginal could express
against the pharmacist, at 261 flagged of 299, is 0.0780. That ceiling is consistent with why the
detector trained on those labels lands where it does; it does not prove the
mechanism, which is named rather than assumed. The resolvable-effect floor
above rests on a power analysis, not an empirical validation of the clinical
instrument, and it does not show that this evaluation's null is free of the
exemplar-selection defect, since the defect-free stratum has only six
positives.

\section{Practical Lesson}
\label{sec:conc-practice}

The lesson this project draws, stated as a lesson rather than as a
demonstrated safeguard, is to check a weak labeller's marginal against a
human-established base rate before training anything on its output, and to
report a simple baseline's own significance test alongside a neural ranking
result rather than assume the ranking statistic is the only one that
matters. The check that this project ran after the fact, illustrated for the
upstream candidate pool by Figure~\ref{fig:exemplar} in
Chapter~\ref{ch:intro}, is cheap relative to the training budget it would
have been read against; that it is cheap is a reason to run it earlier next
time, not a claim that running it here would have changed what this
evaluation could resolve at 12 positives.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conc-future}

\textbf{A larger annotated set.} Every interval in this thesis rests on 12
positives, and the minimum detectable AUROC at that count, 0.7355, sets the
size of the annotation effort a future attempt needs before a modest
detector could be told apart from chance.

\textbf{An independent set for wording-specific signals.} A reversed-direction
zero-shot score ranked above chance under every wording tried but reached the
resolvable floor under none of the three new ones, and its signal sits in the
rows carrying the exemplar defect (Section~\ref{sec:res-rq2}). Whether any
wording carries a real signal can only be tested on pairs it was not found on.

\textbf{A validated exemplar instrument.} Three instruments give $0$,
$0.4745$ and $0.9315$ for how often the exemplar selector showed a pair that
did not carry the conflict it asserted (Section~\ref{sec:r-exemplar}); the
primary estimate, $0.9315$, comes from an extractor never validated against
human annotation. Validating it would establish whether the pool's confirmed
base rate is valid.

\textbf{Frontier-scale local labellers, when feasible.} The comparison this
project needed, a frontier-scale model against the same local labellers on
the same pool, was attempted through a hosted API and withdrawn under the
compliance action Section~\ref{sec:m-ethics} records
(Section~\ref{sec:r-frontier}). Whether the over-calling this chapter
measures attenuates with scale is answerable only by a labeller served
locally, at a scale a hosted channel is not required to reach.

\section{Closing}
\label{sec:conc-closing}

Dangerous medication contradictions between nursing notes and discharge
summaries are real, recurring, and worth detecting. At the size this thesis
could annotate, twelve confirmed contradictions in 299 pairs, the pipeline
built to detect them did not: its own detector is not distinguishable from
chance, the stratum the pool's one identified defect cannot reach cannot
settle whether that null is a defect artifact, and two simple models, one
never trained on the weak labels, find a small precision signal the detector
does not. Local labellers flagged far more pairs than the pharmacist
confirmed, but this thesis does not put one number on how much more, because
that number moves with the prompt.

None of that establishes that the task cannot be done
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-null}). It establishes what a resolvable-effect floor
of 0.7355 lets a 253-pair evaluation say and what it stays silent on, and it
leaves the size and the design of the next attempt to the future work above
rather than to this one.
