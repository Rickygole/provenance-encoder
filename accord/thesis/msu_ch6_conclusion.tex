\chapter{Summary and Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{What Was Asked}
\label{sec:conc-asked}

A nursing note records that a loop diuretic was held because the patient had
become hypovolemic; the discharge summary written five days later instructs
that the same drug be continued at the admission dose. Only one of those
documents leaves the hospital with the patient, and it is not the one
containing the reason the instruction is wrong.

This thesis asked whether that disagreement can be detected automatically
from paired clinical notes in MIMIC-III \citep{johnson2016mimic}, and built a
standard-shaped pipeline to answer it: rule-based candidate mining, language
model weak supervision, and a purpose-built neural detector over a
domain-pretrained clinical encoder. Each stage's reading was tested against a
control, and Section~\ref{sec:conc-found} states what survived that testing.

\section{What Was Found}
\label{sec:conc-found}

With 12 pharmacist-confirmed contradictions in 299 pairs, this evaluation
resolves only large effects, reliably from about an AUROC of 0.75, exactly
0.7355 at 80 percent power (Section~\ref{sec:res-rq4}), a floor checked
against a real score distribution rather than only a synthetic one and not
merely computed by formula. Within that limit, local language-model
labellers flagged far more pairs than the pharmacist confirmed: the deployed
labeller called 261 of the 299 pairs contradictions against 12 confirmed, and
three local eight-billion-parameter models measured on the same pool over-call
by a factor of 15.8196 to 23.5959 against the confirmed rate
(Section~\ref{sec:res-rq2}). How much more depended heavily on the prompt and
on how the 46 ambiguous pairs are read: removing the named conflict from the prompt
moved two of the three models' positive rates down sharply and the third up;
reading the ambiguous pairs as confirmed rather than as negative brings one
model's neutral-prompt ratio to exactly the confirmed
rate, while the least-affected model still over-calls above a factor of four
under every prompt tried. Neither reading is preferred over the other in this
thesis.

The detector trained on those labels ranked at chance, including on the
defect-free rows. On the 253 non-ambiguous pairs the decontaminated ensemble
reaches an AUROC of 0.5145, its bootstrap interval straddling chance; on the
134-pair stratum that the exemplar-selection defect of
Section~\ref{sec:r-exemplar} cannot reach, a defect-free control model still
returns an ensemble AUROC of 0.5469, also straddling chance. A bag-of-words
model trained on the same labels the detector consumed, by contrast,
recovered a small precision signal: its average precision clears its own
no-skill line at $p = 0.0109$ on the full annotated set and $p = 0.0159$ on
the non-ambiguous set, a result its ranking statistic alone does not show and
that does not survive being retrained on the gold labels alone at this sample
size.

\section{What This Establishes, and What It Does Not}
\label{sec:conc-establishes}

The labeller's own marginal fixes an agreement ceiling before a single pair
is scored: holding the confirmed and flagged counts fixed, the largest
Matthews correlation any thresholded label at that marginal could express
against the pharmacist is 0.0768. That ceiling is consistent with why the
detector trained on those labels lands where it does; it does not prove the
mechanism, which is named rather than assumed. The resolvable-effect floor
above was validated against a real score distribution, which certifies that
the class of test this thesis runs resolves an effect where the analytic
calculation says it should; it does not certify that this evaluation's null
is free of the exemplar-selection defect, since a check restricted to the
defect-free stratum has only six positives to work with and can fail to show
the defect is present without showing it is absent.

\section{Practical Lesson}
\label{sec:conc-practice}

The lesson this project draws, stated as a lesson rather than as a
demonstrated safeguard, is to check a weak labeller's marginal against a
human-established base rate before training anything on its output, and to
report a simple baseline's own significance test alongside a neural ranking
result rather than assume the ranking statistic is the only one that
matters. The check that this project ran after the fact, illustrated for the
upstream candidate pool by Figure~\ref{fig:exemplar} in
Chapter~\ref{ch:intro}, is cheap relative to the training budget it would
have been read against; that it is cheap is a reason to run it earlier next
time, not a claim that running it here would have changed what this
evaluation could resolve at 12 positives.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conc-future}

\textbf{A larger annotated set.} Every interval in this thesis rests on 12
positives, and the minimum detectable AUROC at that count, 0.7355, sets the
size of the annotation effort a future attempt needs before a modest
detector could be told apart from chance.

\textbf{The C109 confirmation, if it has not already reported.} A
pre-registered check of whether the reversed-prompt AUROC cell of
Section~\ref{sec:res-rq2} generalises across wording is committed and, as of
this writing, not yet run; until it reports, that cell stays descriptive and
is not treated as a finding in this thesis.

\textbf{A validated exemplar instrument.} Three measurements of how often the
candidate pool's exemplar selector showed the annotator a pair that did not
carry the conflict it asserted disagree with one another
(Section~\ref{sec:r-exemplar}), and none is a direct measurement of the exact
pair the pharmacist saw at scale. Building and validating an instrument that
is, then re-annotating against it, would convert the pool's base rate from an
upper bound into an estimate.

\textbf{Frontier-scale local labellers, when feasible.} The comparison this
project needed, a frontier-scale model against the same local labellers on
the same pool, was attempted through a hosted API and withdrawn under the
compliance action Section~\ref{sec:m-ethics} records
(Section~\ref{sec:r-frontier}). Whether the over-calling this chapter
measures attenuates with scale is answerable only by a labeller served
locally, at a scale a hosted channel is not required to reach.

\section{Closing}
\label{sec:conc-closing}

Dangerous medication contradictions between nursing notes and discharge
summaries are real, recurring, and worth detecting. At the size this thesis
could annotate, twelve confirmed contradictions in 299 pairs, the pipeline
built to detect them did not: its own detector ranks at chance on both the
full evaluation and the stratum the pool's one identified defect cannot
reach, while a plain bag-of-words model trained on the identical weak labels
finds a small precision signal the neural ranking statistic does not. Local
labellers flagged far more pairs than the pharmacist confirmed, but this
thesis does not put one number on how much more, because that number moves
with the prompt and with how the ambiguous pairs are read, and reports both
rather than choosing one for the reader.

None of that establishes that the task cannot be done
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-null}). It establishes what a resolvable-effect floor
of 0.7355 lets a 253-pair evaluation say and what it stays silent on, and it
leaves the size and the design of the next attempt to the future work above
rather than to this one.
