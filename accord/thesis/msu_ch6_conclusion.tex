\chapter{Summary and Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{What Was Asked}
\label{sec:conc-asked}

A nursing note records that a loop diuretic was held because the patient had
become hypovolemic. The discharge summary written five days later instructs that
the same drug be continued at the admission dose. Only one of those documents
leaves the hospital with the patient, and it is not the one containing the
reason the instruction is wrong.

This thesis asked whether that disagreement can be detected automatically from
paired clinical notes in MIMIC-III \citep{johnson2016mimic}, and built a
standard-shaped pipeline to
answer it: rule-based candidate mining, language model weak supervision, and a
purpose-built neural detector over a domain-pretrained clinical encoder. Five
questions were posed, four about the stages and one about the field that
publishes pipelines like this one. Every stage failed a control, and not every
failure carries the same weight, a distinction Section~\ref{sec:conc-found}
keeps rather than collapses.

\section{What Was Found}
\label{sec:conc-found}

\textbf{RQ1, candidate generation.} Four hand written rules enrich the candidate
space to $12$ confirmed contradictions in $299$ pharmacist annotated pairs, a
base rate of $0.0401$. That is a real enrichment over an unfiltered background
rate orders of magnitude lower, and it is nowhere near usable precision. The
finding that matters more than the rate is the exemplar defect: the mechanism
choosing which sentence pair to display was independent of the mechanism
deciding the candidate was a conflict. On the $165$-row Rule 1 and Rule 2 gold
stratum ($134 + 31$), two measurements disagree and both are kept: a
reconstruction, calibrated at $0.8934$ to $0.9508$ fidelity because the
original row order cannot be recovered exactly, resolves $137$ of the $165$
rows and finds $65$ of them wrong, a rate of $0.4745$, with $28$ unresolved;
an exact join, covering only $23$ of the $165$ rows, finds none wrong. The
annotator, the weak labeller and the model all reasoned about whichever
evidence each candidate happened to receive, which makes $0.0401$ an upper
bound on annotation validity rather than a clean estimate of pool precision,
and the wrong rate itself is a range, $0$ to $0.4745$, not a single number.

Comparing exposed and unexposed strata does not settle it either. Rules 1 and 2
account for $165$ pairs with $6$ positives, a rate of $0.0364$; rules 3 and 4
account for $134$ pairs with $6$ positives, a rate of $0.0448$.
Section~\ref{sec:res-rq1} reports the Fisher exact test on that split: an odds
ratio of $0.805$ at $p = 0.7725$, achieved power $0.0576$, and a minimum
detectable rate difference of $0.0895$, more than twice the overall base rate.
The strata are indistinguishable at this sample size, which is not the same as
equal.

\textbf{RQ2, weak supervision.} The weak labeller assigned the contradiction
class to $261$ of the $299$ annotated pairs, against $12$ true positives in the
same set. The detector distilled from those labels predicts contradiction for
$278$ of the $299$. A labeller positive on the large majority of a pool whose
true positive rate is $0.0401$ carries almost no information about which pairs
are contradictions, and the student inherited the marginal rather than the
judgment. Neither of the two standard corrections was available: the adjusted
count that would fix a biased prevalence estimate needs the labeller's error
rates on the same distribution \citep{forman2008quantifying}, and the
doubly robust correction for surrogate label bias needs a gold-labelled
subsample \citep{egami2023surrogates}, which is the resource the workflow
existed to avoid spending. Substitution fails at every scale tested, but not by
the same amount, and the amount is ordered. A frontier labeller on the $253$
non-ambiguous pairs flagged $48$ and was right about $9$, which is $4.00$ times
that set's confirmed rate, and a second reached $5.00$ times, against
the $15.8196$ to $23.5959$ range the local 8B labellers produced on the candidate
pool. Those are different denominators, stated so in
Table~\ref{tab:overcalling}. On one denominator and one prompt the seven labellers
measured for it fall in capability order, and two pre-registered runs separate the
factors instead of arguing about them: changing only scale within one family, one
maker and one serving channel moves the quantity by $2.0635$, and changing only the
serving channel moves it by $1.0962$ (Section~\ref{sec:r-capability}). The ordering
is therefore a property of capability rather than of who served the model or where,
and it holds at a fixed prompt only: changing the prompt moves one of those models
by $1.6800$ times, further than the channel does. A third labeller's rows have
since been withdrawn, for the reason Section~\ref{sec:m-ethics} gives. The
magnitude tracks the labeller; what does not move is that no labeller tested
is calibrated at this base rate.

\textbf{RQ3, architecture.} None of the proposed components contributes what it
was credited with, and the controls establish this positively rather than by
absence of evidence. The authority parameters reported as learned finish at
$0.5076 \pm 0.0006$ and $1.0038 \pm 0.0010$ across seeds, against initialisation
at exactly $0.5$ and $1.0$; a parameter agreeing to two decimals across
independent seeds, within two percent of where it started, was not discovered by
training. The harm head emits scores spanning $0.3977$ to $0.6096$, the range of
an untrained sigmoid, and its Spearman correlation with the pharmacist's danger
rating over the $299$ pairs is $-0.0001$ at $p = 0.9991$. That null measures an
untrained module and says nothing about whether clinical severity is
predictable. On a second system, a provenance-conditioned encoder built for this
analysis and not the clinical detector above, a single-seed randomised control
over the same role-label alphabet directionally loses to true role labels by
$0.3277$ nats with no significance claim attached, while an arbitrary
positional ordinal, a different feature rather than a randomised version of
the same one, beats both: role semantics look like they carry something real
on this seed, and a provenance-free position looks like it carries more. The
comparison is
the randomisation sanity check \citep{adebayo2018sanity} and the control task
\citep{hewitt2019control}, adapted to a different kind of component and, in the
randomised arm, departing from their per-type requirement by sampling per
occurrence; neither design is claimed here.

\textbf{RQ4, evaluation.} Three configurations sit at chance. On the
$253$ non-ambiguous pairs with $12$ positives, the decontaminated three seed ensemble
reaches AUROC $0.5145$ with Mann-Whitney $p = 0.8668$, and the per seed mean is
$0.5211 \pm 0.0201$. On the $299$ annotated pairs with the same $12$ positives,
under subject-disjoint splits, the three seed mean AUROC is $0.4967 \pm 0.0350$,
and a DeLong test between the control model's seed ensemble and ACCORD's
contaminated released run, which is the correlated comparison the two share
their cases with \citep{delong1988comparing}, gives $p = 0.930$. Under that
contaminated run, and not under the decontaminated ensemble above, the score
distributions on the $253$-pair core overlap: positives average $0.4198$ and
negatives $0.4282$, with Mann-Whitney $p = 0.6418$, so the positives score
marginally lower. A TF-IDF logistic regression on the $299$ annotated pairs reaches
AUROC $0.5761$ and average precision $0.1571$, above both neural
configurations, and whether that is surface form signal or small sample noise is
not established.

At the operating threshold the same conclusion appears as a cost, and the
direction of it is forced by where the detector sits rather than a separate
finding. On the $253$ pair core the decontaminated detector, in the first
seed, flags
$216 + 9 = 225$ pairs, predicting positive for nearly the whole pool; $9$ are
correct, $216$ wrong, $25$ true negatives and $3$ missed. Its ranking is at
chance: Cohen $\kappa$ across seeds is $-0.0105$, expected calibration error is
$0.8015$, and the ensemble AUROC's bootstrap interval is $[0.3210, 0.7034]$.
One consequence of that operating point is that precision, $0.0424 \pm 0.0023$,
sits below the pool base rate of $0.0474$ while recall is
$0.7778 \pm 0.0393$; the margin is $0.0051$, smaller than
the binomial standard error on $12$ of $253$ pairs, $0.0134$, so it is read as an illustration of
the operating point rather than as evidence on its own.

\textbf{RQ5, practice.} On a $75$ paper corpus deliberately enriched toward work
that attends to selection, $4$ papers audit the selection step under the strict
reading and $14$ under the permissive one, across $502$ of $525$ coded fields
verified against their sources, the remaining $23$ being codings of absence
that carry no quote by construction (Section~\ref{sec:res-rq5}). A blind second coder on $19$ overlapping papers gives
$\kappa = 0.855$ at $0.9474$ agreement on the permissive field and
$\kappa = 0.730$ at $0.7895$ on null attribute handling. On the strict field
both coders returned the same value on all $19$ papers, so agreement is $1.0$
and $\kappa$ is undefined; it is reported as undefined, not as perfect. The
enrichment runs against the finding: a corpus over sampling careful work still
yields $4$ of $75$. The check being counted is not an unreasonable one to ask
for. Stage level auditing has been demonstrated for fairness in tabular
preprocessing pipelines \citep{biswas2021fairpreprocessing}, and the reason a
selection step needs auditing before its outputs are interpreted has been argued
directly \citep{pfohl2025disaggregated}.

\section{What the Controls Changed}
\label{sec:conc-controls}

Without controls, everything above reads as a small underpowered study that
found nothing. The controls convert the same results into measurements with a
stated resolution.

The positive control carries the most weight. The device adapts one
observational epidemiology formalised decades ago
\citep{lipsitch2010negative}: run the analysis where the answer is known, and
read the result as a statement about the instrument. Run on AG News with the
identical training harness and an adjudication rule committed before any run,
the resolved pretraining benefit is $4.53$ accuracy points at convergence; the
same harness, on the in-hospital mortality task built for this control,
returns a null, $+0.0031$. What that pairing licenses, and does not, for the
contradiction evaluation's own sensitivity is argued in
Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion}, Section~\ref{sec:disc-null}, not repeated here.
The general proposition that a pretraining objective predicts downstream
utility poorly is not established here; it is established elsewhere and at a
scale this project cannot approach \citep{lourie2025scaling,feng2026tessera}.
The sweep is a control, not a contribution.

The decontamination control changed the headline. Excluding the evaluation
identifiers at load time moved AUROC on the same $253$ pairs from $0.4595$ to an
ensemble $0.5145$, a move of $0.0550$ that Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion} reports as
untested and that lands nowhere useful in either direction. The value of the
control is not that move. It is that the number reported before it was
contaminated, and nothing in the metrics said so.

The sign provenance control changed no value and several conclusions.
Retrofitting signed storage for quantities persisted through an absolute value
reproduced every prior figure exactly, maximum discrepancy $0.0$, while
reversing seven of the fourteen comparable drift slopes. The demographic parity
slope is $-0.0078$ signed under one selection regime and $+0.0118$ under the
other, and both invert when stored as magnitudes. A quantity stored as a
magnitude licenses no directional claim.

The randomisation controls changed the sign of the architecture argument on
the second system, not on the clinical detector, but not through the arm built
for that purpose: the pre-specified role-label control ran underpowered
(RQ3 above), not falsified. The sharper result is
the segment-index arm, an arbitrary ordinal carrying no provenance meaning,
which reduces the loss by $0.9476$ nats against true role labels' $0.8244$
nats: a substitute with no provenance content outperforming the credited
component is not consistent with that component working as credited, the way
a mere ablation drop would be. Chapter~\ref{ch:results} reports both comparisons, which is why RQ3
is answered negatively rather than left open.

The controls were cheap. Logged control compute totals $1216.4$ GPU minutes across $79$ runs: the
AG News convergence sweep $555$ minutes over $48$ runs, a separate
single-budget comparison of the same four arms $34.9$ minutes over $12$ runs,
the clinical comparison $612.3$ minutes over $17$ runs, and the two signed
storage re-runs $0.7$ and $13.5$ minutes. Person hours were never logged, so
this is a compute only claim.

\section{Things to Check If You Are Building This}
\label{sec:conc-practice}

These are the checks that, had they been run when they were available, would
each have changed what was reported here.

Check that the evidence a candidate is displayed with is the evidence that
triggered it. The failure mode is the one drawn back in
Figure~\ref{fig:exemplar}: a rule that quantifies over a set of medication
mentions, an exemplar selector that indexes into that set positionally, and
nothing in between forcing the two to agree. The check is an assertion over two
data structures, and on the Rule 1 and Rule 2 gold stratum it is the check that
would have caught the largest upstream defect in this project before any
annotation was commissioned, a wrong rate as high as $0.4745$ (RQ1 above).

Estimate the base rate on a human labelled sample before generating weak labels,
and print the labeller's marginal beside it. A labeller positive on $261$ of
$299$ pairs against a base rate of $0.0401$ is diagnosable in one line when the
labels are produced, not after a detector has been trained on them, and the
dependence of agreement statistics on the marginals has been documented since
1990 \citep{feinstein1990paradoxes,byrt1993pabak}.

Report precision against the base rate of the pool the system ran on, with the
denominator stated, and prefer a coefficient that uses all four confusion matrix
cells when the classes are this skewed \citep{chicco2020mcc}. Precision of
$0.0424$ is a different object depending on
whether the pool's base rate is $0.005$ or $0.0474$, and here it is the latter.
At an operating point where the detector flags nearly the whole pool, precision
falling below base rate follows from the arithmetic of that operating point and
should be read beside kappa and the ranking metrics, not asserted alone.

Run a randomised counterpart of any component reported as learned, under the
same training conditions, and log the distance a learned parameter moved from
its initialisation. A parameter that finishes at $0.5076$ from a start of $0.5$,
agreeing to two decimals across seeds, is reporting its initialisation.

Run a positive control on the instrument before reporting a null from it, and
report achieved power alongside any comparison that returns no difference. The
positive control here, the pre-registered convergence sweep, cost $555$ minutes
over $48$ runs, and the strata comparison has $p = 0.7725$ at achieved power
$0.0576$, where the first number
without the second reads as evidence of no effect.

Keep the denominators separate and state them with every metric. The $299$
annotated pairs and the $253$ non-ambiguous pairs are different sets, and
conflating them in this project inverted a central claim that then survived
several rounds of internal review.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conc-future}

Three pieces of work follow directly from the limitations. Each needs a
resource this project did not have: the annotator's time, an annotation
budget, and a controlled simulator. A fourth item, a within-channel capability
test that needed neither, was struck because it was run: Section~\ref{sec:r-capability}
reports it, and the confound it addressed turns out to be worth about ten percent
against a capability step of roughly two.

\textbf{Re-annotate against corrected exemplars.} The base rate of $0.0401$
remains an upper bound rather than an estimate (RQ1 above). By reconstruction
the exemplar matched the trigger
for $72$ of the $137$ resolved Rule 1 and Rule 2 candidates, a rate of
$0.5255$; by an exact join covering far fewer rows, all $23$ of the $23$ it can
resolve match. A separate join across all four rules matches $35$ rows of the
annotated sample to the rebuilt candidates table
(Section~\ref{sec:res-rq1}), a different operation with a different
denominator, not a third reading of the same exemplar-correctness question. Rebuilding the
selector so the displayed pair is the pair that fired the rule, then
re-annotating the same candidate identifiers, would establish how much of the
measured base rate is real. The rules and the pool exist and the defect is
located, so this is bounded work.

\textbf{Assemble a cohort with more positives.} Every interval here rests on
$12$ positives. The minimum detectable AUROC at that count is $0.7355$ at
$80\%$ power and $\alpha = 0.05$, and the smallest stratum difference the
exemplar comparison could detect is $0.0895$ in absolute rate. A cohort large
enough to resolve effects where a useful clinical system would operate is the
single change that would most alter what can be concluded, and it means
annotation budget rather than modelling effort.

\textbf{Explain the selection channel anomaly.} Two selection channels on one
population with baseline disparity held fixed produce demographic parity drift
slopes of opposite sign, $-0.0078$ and $+0.0118$, and a controlled simulator
sweep inverts rather than reproduces the mapping. The pattern is stable and
reproducible and has no explanation. Five candidate mechanisms were proposed and
refuted inside this project; the sixth is left to work that can hold more of the
generating process fixed than this design allows.



\section{Closing}
\label{sec:conc-closing}

Dangerous medication contradictions between nursing notes and discharge
summaries are real, recurring, and worth detecting. A standard-shaped pipeline
built for them does not detect them, and the sharpest thing this thesis can say
is not that sentence but the comparison beside it. On the $253$ non-ambiguous
pairs, the pipeline's detector reaches a Matthews correlation of $-0.0070$ and a
separately prompted Llama 3 8B evaluation baseline $-0.0436$, both intervals
containing chance, while two frontier labellers given one zero-shot prompt and no
training at all reach $+0.3189$ and $+0.2253$, both intervals excluding chance. The stronger of the two is
also separated from the pipeline: Sonnet's interval begins at $+0.1473$, above the
detector's upper bound of $+0.0753$. Haiku's begins at $+0.0688$, which overlaps it, so
the claim that a frontier labeller outranks this pipeline rests on the Sonnet row alone.
Section~\ref{sec:m-stats} commits this thesis to claiming an ordering among systems only
on an interval around the difference, and no such interval was computed; the
non-overlap above is the weaker evidence that is available, and the ordering claim is
scoped to it
(Table~\ref{tab:mcc}). The zero-shot run flags $48$ of $253$ and is right about
$9$, a precision of $0.1875$ against a base rate of $0.0474$ at a recall of
$0.7500$, and its false-positive rate on the clear negatives is $0.1618$ against
the detector's $0.9253$.

Those systems saw the same rows, the same exemplars, the same base rate, the same
twelve positives and the same single annotator. What that rules out is narrower than
it first appears, and the narrower version is what this thesis claims. It rules out
the evaluation being unable to resolve an effect at this sample size, and it rules
out the base rate and the exemplar defect making the task unresolvable, because both
applied in full to the systems that resolved it. It does not rule out rarity as a
cause of this pipeline's failure, because rarity reaches this pipeline through a
learned label distribution the prompted systems never had: the labeller's degenerate
marginal is the reason the detector cannot rank, and that marginal is a response to
prevalence. Nor does it rule out the annotator, whose ambiguous calls are the pairs
the more capable labellers most over-flag, so the annotator is not a constant across
the comparison in the way the other two are. What the evidence does isolate is that
the failure is not a property of the task as posed, and that this pipeline's
stage-wise metrics reported health while it was failing. Whether the decisive defect
is the label distribution or the detector's construction is not settled here, because
no arm of this thesis trains a detector of this shape on non-degenerate labels. The practical
recommendation that follows is one sentence. Before building a pipeline of this shape for a rare clinical
event, prompt a frontier model on the evaluation set and make the pipeline beat it.
This project did not do that, and doing it would have cost an afternoon at the start
rather than a thesis at the end. Rule based mining produces a
pool at $0.0401$, a property of applying high-recall rules to an event this
rare, and weak supervision produces a labeller positive on $261$ of $299$
pairs against $12$ true positives, a property of asking an instruction-tuned
model to flag a rare class at low prevalence; both should recur in direction in a
differently built version of this pipeline, and neither magnitude should be
carried across, because frontier labellers asked the same question on the same
pairs were positive on $64$ and $75$ of $299$ rather than $261$. What would not necessarily recur
belongs to this build specifically: between $0$ and $0.4745$ of
resolved Rule 1 and Rule 2 candidates carry
the wrong exemplar because this project's selector indexed positionally, and those
two endpoints are two incompatible measurements rather than the ends of one interval
(Section~\ref{sec:res-rq1}), the
architectural components report their initialisations because of how this
project's parameters were wired, and a second system shows a
provenance-free position beating both true role semantics and a randomised
control drawn from the same label alphabet. The detector sits at chance on
both evaluations, an AUROC indistinguishable from $0.5$ against a minimum
detectable AUROC of $0.7355$ at $12$ positives, and that result is the one
finding in this list large enough to answer RQ4 regardless of which category
the rest sit in. Operating at precision $0.0424$ below a base rate of $0.0474$
illustrates the same point rather than adding independent evidence, since the
margin between precision and base rate, $0.0051$, is smaller than the
binomial standard error on $12$ of $253$ pairs, $0.0134$.

None of that establishes that the task cannot be done, a limit stated once
and not re-argued here (Section~\ref{sec:disc-null}). What it establishes is
narrower and more useful: each failure has a located mechanism, and each was
separated from measurement artifact by a control that fired.

A negative result that has been diagnosed is worth more than one that has not. Part of that diagnosis, in this project, was a set of controls that belong to other people's papers
\citep{lipsitch2010negative,adebayo2018sanity,hewitt2019control,egami2023surrogates,feinstein1990paradoxes},
cost $1216.4$ GPU minutes in total, and had not been run on this pipeline before this work; running someone else's instrument is not inventing it. The rest of what this project leaves
behind is three checks of its own, named for what each one does: trigger-exemplar dissociation, the degeneracy precondition, and sign provenance. What the three share is that each is
cheap, each is a one-line assertion or a pre-registered gate, and each catches a defect that no reported metric here was sensitive to. Neither half is a new method, and this thesis
does not claim one; both are small, transferable checks, offered together so the next attempt starts further along than this one did.
