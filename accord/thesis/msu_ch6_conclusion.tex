\chapter{Summary and Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{What Was Asked}
\label{sec:conc-asked}

A nursing note records that a loop diuretic was held because the patient had
become hypovolemic; the discharge summary written five days later instructs
that the same drug be continued at the admission dose. Only one of those
documents leaves the hospital with the patient, and it is not the one
containing the reason the instruction is wrong.

This thesis asked whether that disagreement can be detected automatically
from paired clinical notes in MIMIC-III \citep{johnson2016mimic}, and built a
standard-shaped pipeline to answer it: rule-based candidate mining, language
model weak supervision, and a purpose-built neural detector over a
domain-pretrained clinical encoder. Each stage's reading was checked, and Section~\ref{sec:conc-found} states what survived that testing.

\section{What Was Found}
\label{sec:conc-found}

With 12 pharmacist-confirmed contradictions in 299 pairs, this evaluation
resolves only large AUROC effects, stated per decision rule: a power analysis
on a real score distribution puts 80 percent power at 0.7466 for a two-sided
Mann-Whitney test and 0.8084 for the registered decision rule, which has power
of only 0.52075 at the analytic minimum detectable AUROC of 0.7355
(Section~\ref{sec:res-rq4}). The floor is about AUROC; separately, a baseline's
average precision exceeded its own permutation null, on a statistic chosen
after its value was known, as reported below. Within that limit, every 8B
labeller under the clinical-pharmacist framing flagged several times the
confirmed count when ambiguous pairs are counted negative: the deployed
labeller called 261 of the 299 pairs contradictions against 12 confirmed, and
three local models under the replication prompt
over-call the same pool by a factor of 15.8196 to 23.5959 against the
confirmed rate (Section~\ref{sec:res-rq2}). How much more depended heavily on
the prompt: a clinical-pharmacist framing prompt, whose differences from a
plain yes/no prompt are confounded, gave higher positive rates for
granite3.3:8b and qwen3:8b; the llama3:8b shift lies within the gap between
two runs of the same prompt text and is not interpreted. Counting the 46 ambiguous
pairs as confirmed is a post hoc rescaling that multiplies every ratio by
12/58 and says nothing about agreement; under it \texttt{qwen3:8b} and
\texttt{granite3.3:8b} under the plain prompt fall to 1.0000 and 1.8448. The
local Fisher tests, run under the frontier template prompts, did not show
that the labellers flag ambiguous pairs more than clear negatives, which with
46 ambiguous pairs is absence of evidence, not evidence against. The margin is also fragile: relabelling 5
flagged pairs would bring the lowest hard-label ratio under the ambiguous-negative reading,
4.8333, below the
highest ratio measured on public text.

The detector trained on those labels was not distinguishable from chance
on the 253 non-ambiguous pairs: the decontaminated ensemble reaches an AUROC
of 0.5145, its bootstrap interval excluding values above 0.7034, and its
average precision, 0.0598, is not above its permutation null ($p = 0.4629$).
On the 108 non-ambiguous Rules 3 and 4 pairs the exemplar-selection defect of
Section~\ref{sec:r-exemplar} cannot reach, the same ensemble reaches 0.6095
($p = 0.1910$, 95 percent bootstrap interval 0.3029 to 0.9010), above the
0.4221 on Rules 1 and 2, the direction the defect-artifact reading predicts.
The pre-registered Outcome A means only that this stratum fell within 0.10 of
the full-set AUROC and was not significant, a margin of 0.0950 that turns on
about one positive; with 6 positives and a minimum detectable AUROC of 0.8064
this fails to show the null is a defect artifact and cannot establish that it
is not. Average precision, a statistic chosen after its value was known,
gives a narrower result. A bag-of-words model trained on the weak labels
clears its no-skill line at $p = 0.0109$ on the full annotated set and
$p = 0.0159$ on the 253 non-ambiguous pairs (C107) and holds in 11 of 12
leave-one-positive-out drops; an inference model never trained on the weak
labels is also above its own permutation null on the non-ambiguous set
($p = 0.01335$, C111), and the detector ensemble is not. No paired permutation test
on within-system ranks separates any two of the three (bag-of-words against
the detector, $p = 0.17875$; inference model against the detector,
$p = 0.1452$), so neither simple model is shown to find a signal the detector
lacks. That is an average-precision signal a label-free model also finds,
which C111's registered interpretation calls a surface lexical or
semantic-similarity signal, not evidence that the weak labels carried signal.

\section{What This Establishes, and What It Does Not}
\label{sec:conc-establishes}

The labeller's own marginal fixes an agreement ceiling before a single pair
is scored: holding the confirmed and flagged counts fixed, the largest
Matthews correlation any thresholded label at that marginal could express
against the pharmacist, at 261 flagged of 299, is 0.0780. The ceiling bounds
thresholded labels, not a ranking: it is a precondition to check before
training, not an explanation of the detector's null. The resolvable-effect floor
above rests on a power analysis, not an empirical validation of the clinical
instrument, and it does not show that this evaluation's null is free of the
exemplar-selection defect, since the defect-free stratum has only six
positives.

\section{Practical Lesson}
\label{sec:conc-practice}

The lesson this project draws, stated as a lesson rather than as a
demonstrated safeguard, is to check a weak labeller's marginal against a
human-established base rate before training anything on its output, and to
report a simple baseline's own significance test alongside a neural ranking
result rather than assume the ranking statistic is the only one that
matters. The check that this project ran after the fact, illustrated for the
upstream candidate pool by Figure~\ref{fig:exemplar} in
Chapter~\ref{ch:intro}, is cheap relative to the training budget it would
have been read against; that it is cheap is a reason to run it earlier next
time, not a claim that running it here would have changed what this
evaluation could resolve at 12 positives.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conc-future}

\textbf{A larger annotated set.} Every interval in this thesis rests on 12
positives, and the per-rule floors at that count, 0.7466 for a two-sided
Mann-Whitney test and 0.8084 for the registered rule, set the size of the
annotation effort a future attempt needs before a modest
detector could be told apart from chance.

\textbf{An independent set for wording-specific signals.} A reversed-direction
zero-shot score from llama3:8b ranked above chance at the uncorrected 0.05 level under every
wording tried, cleared the registered Bonferroni threshold under only one of
the three new wordings, and reached the
registered 0.7355 threshold under none of the three new ones, a rule with power
of only 0.52075 at that value, and the llama3:8b signal sits in the
rows carrying the exemplar defect (Section~\ref{sec:res-rq2}). The descriptive
granite3.3:8b C109 cells rank above chance under every wording and meet both
registered criteria under two of the three new wordings, one by a narrow AUROC margin; its best wording,
picked after the fact, most strongly on the defect-free Rules 3 and 4 (0.8775,
6 positives). Whether a graded score escapes the agreement ceiling is not
settled, and that result is the strongest reason not to read the detector's
ranking null as evidence the task is unsolvable. A wording-specific signal can
only be tested on pairs it was not found on.

\textbf{A validated exemplar instrument.} Three instruments give $0$,
$0.4745$ and $0.9315$ for how often the exemplar selector showed a pair that
did not carry the conflict it asserted (Section~\ref{sec:r-exemplar}), and
none establishes the rate; the highest, $0.9315$, comes from an extractor never
validated against human annotation and may be an artifact of it. Validating it would establish whether the pool's confirmed
base rate is valid.

\textbf{Frontier-scale local labellers, when feasible.} The comparison this
project needed, a frontier-scale model against the same local labellers on
the same pool, was attempted through a hosted API and withdrawn under the
compliance action Section~\ref{sec:m-ethics} records
(Section~\ref{sec:r-frontier}). Whether the over-calling this chapter
measures attenuates with scale is answerable only by a labeller served
locally, at a scale a hosted channel is not required to reach.

\section{Closing}
\label{sec:conc-closing}

Dangerous medication contradictions between nursing notes and discharge
summaries are real, recurring, and worth detecting. At the size this thesis
could annotate, twelve confirmed contradictions in 299 pairs, the pipeline
built to detect them did not, and the thesis leaves three results that need
no statistical power: a stage-wise control audit in which the first three
stages each reported a number that its control or code audit did not
support; an agreement ceiling,
computed from the weak labeller's marginal alone, of 0.0780 at 261 flagged of
299, a check to run before any training; and trigger-exemplar dissociation, a
mining-bug class caught by a one-line assertion. In support, the detector
shows no AUROC effect above about 0.75 to 0.81 on the 253 non-ambiguous pairs,
the defect-free stratum cannot settle whether that null is a defect artifact,
and a bag-of-words model and a model never trained on the weak labels both
find an average-precision signal that no paired test
separates from the detector. The planted-defect benchmark, a synthetic mirror-image check of
the Rule 1/2 versus Rule 3/4 Fisher test rather than a validation of it, did
not reach its required firing rate, a limitation this thesis discloses
rather than a result it claims.

None of that establishes that the task cannot be done
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-null}). It establishes what a per-rule floor of 0.7466
to 0.8084 lets a 253-pair evaluation say and what it stays silent on, and it
leaves the size and the design of the next attempt to the future work above
rather than to this one.
